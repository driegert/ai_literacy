What an LLM Actually Does

The Whole Trick: Predict the Next Token

An LLM is a function that, given the text so far, outputs a probability distribution over what comes next — then samples from it. Repeat.

That’s it. Everything else — “reasoning,” “writing code,” “knowing facts” — is this loop running over and over.

This is a conditional distribution.

\[ P(\text{next token} \mid \text{all previous tokens}) \]

How It “Reads”: Tokens

The model doesn’t see words or letters — it sees tokens: subword chunks.

  • "statistician" might split into stat + ist + ician.
  • "unbelievable" might be un + believ + able.
  • Common words = one token; rare words = several.
  • Numbers, whitespace, and punctuation are tokens too — which is why models can be oddly bad at arithmetic.

Tokens \(\to\) a Distribution You Can Sample

The model gives every candidate next token a score which is turned into probabilities; temperature controls how “peaked” the distribution is:

  • Low temperature \(\to\) spiky \(\to\) picks the most likely token (“greedy”).
    • Deterministic when temperature = 0
  • High temperature \(\to\) flat \(\to\) takes more risks (“creative” / hallucination-prone).

Where do the scores come from? From data.
(A lot of data.)

Two Phases of Training

Phase What happens Analogy
Pre-training Predict the next token over ~the whole internet. Unsupervised, enormously expensive. Learns the shape of language
Post-training Instruction tuning + RLHF (human/AI feedback). Cheaper, shapes behaviour. Learns to be helpful and follow instructions

Key point: pre-training builds a raw next-token engine; post-training makes it answer you instead of just autocompleting.

Fine-tuning is post-training you do yourself: take a released model and keep training it on your data (your grading style, your domain) to specialize its behaviour — far cheaper than pre-training from scratch.

“Modes”: Fast vs. Reasoning

Often the same model, but a different amount of computation per answer:

  • Fast mode — answers immediately. Great for quick, low-stakes tasks.
  • Reasoning / extended mode — the model first generates a long internal “thinking” trace (more tokens, more time, more cost) before answering. Better on multi-step problems.

We often do not see the generated intermediate tokens (the “thinking trace”). What we see is the final output.

Mechanical Takeaway

LLMs generates.
It does not retrieve.

A fluent answer is a high-probability answer — not a true one. That gap is why we have to be careful when evaluating what an LLM gives us for a response.
